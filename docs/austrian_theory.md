# Austrian Business Cycle Theory Guide

## Overview

The Austrian Business Cycle Theory (ABCT) explains economic cycles through the lens of credit expansion and market distortions created by central bank interventions.

## Key Principles

### 1. Natural Interest Rate
- The market naturally determines interest rates based on time preference and capital availability
- Central bank interference creates artificial rates that don't reflect true market conditions

### 2. Credit Expansion Phase
- Artificially low interest rates encourage excessive borrowing
- Entrepreneurs make investments that appear profitable only because of cheap credit
- These "malinvestments" would not be sustainable at natural interest rates

### 3. Capital Structure Distortion
- Cheap credit distorts the temporal structure of production
- Too many resources flow to long-term, capital-intensive projects
- Consumer goods production becomes relatively underfunded

### 4. The Inevitable Correction
- Malinvestments become apparent as market forces reassert themselves
- Credit tightens as the unsustainability becomes evident
- Liquidation of bad investments causes recession

## Indicators We Monitor

### NFCI (National Financial Conditions Index)
**What it measures**: Composite of 105 financial indicators including:
- Interest rates and spreads
- Credit measures
- Stock market volatility
- Dollar exchange rates

**Austrian interpretation**:
- Negative values indicate artificially loose financial conditions
- Declining trend shows increasing credit expansion
- Combination signals dangerous distortion of natural market signals

### Private Credit Growth
**What it measures**: Total credit extended to private non-financial sector

**Austrian interpretation**:
- Rapid growth indicates artificial credit expansion
- Stagnation/decline suggests approaching end of cycle
- Credit expansion creates the boom; credit contraction reveals the bust

### Treasury Yield Curve
**What it measures**: 
- 2-year Treasury yield trends
- 10-year minus 2-year spread

**Austrian interpretation**:
- Yield curve inversion historically precedes recessions
- 2-year yield peaking and declining shows cycle turning
- Market pricing in future economic slowdown as malinvestments unwind

## Key Concepts

### Malinvestment
Investments that appear profitable only because of artificially cheap credit but become unsustainable when natural market forces reassert themselves.

Examples:
- Overbuilding in real estate during easy credit
- Unprofitable tech companies during low interest rate periods
- Excessive corporate debt used for financial engineering

### Liquidation
The necessary process of eliminating malinvestments and reallocating resources to productive uses. While painful, this process is essential for genuine economic recovery.

### Time Preference
The natural human preference for present goods over future goods, which determines natural interest rates in an unhampered market.

## Implications for Investors

### During Credit Expansion
- Be cautious of investments dependent on continued cheap credit
- Look for signs of malinvestment in various sectors
- Consider hard assets (gold, bitcoin) as hedges against monetary distortion

### During Correction Phase
- Expect liquidation of overleveraged positions
- Cash and bonds may outperform
- Look for genuine investment opportunities after the correction

### Long-term Perspective
- Austrian theory suggests cycles are inevitable under central banking
- Focus on businesses with real economic value, not financial engineering
- Understand that corrections, while painful, restore economic health
