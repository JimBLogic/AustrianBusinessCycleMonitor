## Key Austrian Concepts (Quick Reference)

### Methodology
- **Praxeology**: Science of human action; economics derived from logical reasoning
- **Methodological Individualism**: All social phenomena explained through individual actions
- **A Priori Knowledge**: Economic laws derived through logic, not empirical testing

### Core Economic Principles
- **Subjective Value Theory**: Value determined by individual preferences, not objective properties
- **Marginal Utility**: Additional satisfaction from consuming one more unit decreases
- **Opportunity Cost**: Value of the best alternative forgone (term coined by Wieser)
- **Time Preference**: People prefer present goods to future goods

### Business Cycle Theory
1. **Credit Expansion**: Central banks artificially lower interest rates
2. **Malinvestment**: Cheap credit leads to unsustainable business projects
3. **Market Signals Distorted**: Prices no longer reflect real supply and demand
4. **Inevitable Correction**: Market forces eventually reassert, causing recession
5. **Liquidation Necessary**: Bad investments must be cleared out for recovery

### Key Figures Quick Facts

**Carl Menger (1840-1921)**
- Founded Austrian School (1871)
- Developed subjective value theory
- Established methodological foundation

**Ludwig von Mises (1881-1973)**  
- Developed praxeology methodology
- Proved impossibility of socialist calculation (1920)
- Created Austrian business cycle theory
- Masterwork: "Human Action" (1949)

**Friedrich Hayek (1899-1992)**
- Nobel Prize winner (1974)
- Explained how prices communicate knowledge
- Warned against central planning in "The Road to Serfdom"
- Developed concept of spontaneous order

**Murray Rothbard (1926-1995)**
- Extended Austrian theory to anarcho-capitalism
- Combined economics with natural rights philosophy
- Comprehensive treatise: "Man, Economy, and State" (1962)
- Applied Austrian theory to American economic history
